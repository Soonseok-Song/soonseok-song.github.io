Resistance Hydrodynamics Laboratory

We study the hydrodynamics of ships and marine renewable energy devices, combining high-fidelity CFD with towing-tank and flow-channel experiments.

About the laboratory

We are a ship hydrodynamics group. Computational fluid dynamics is our primary tool, and physical experiment is how we keep it honest.

Our work asks how a hull interacts with water and what that interaction costs: the resistance a ship meets, how it moves and steers in waves, how it behaves among ice floes, and how much of that energy can be recovered through air lubrication or wind-assisted propulsion. Much of our effort goes into the step between model and full scale — surface roughness, unlike a hull form, cannot simply be scaled up, so a model test alone cannot answer the question.

Marine renewable energy is a second major strand of our work: the performance and structural loading of tidal current turbines, and the coupled behaviour of floating offshore wind turbines under wave and mooring loads. Our CFD capability further extends to environmental problems such as dissolved-oxygen transport in aquaculture and scour around bridge piers.

We hold that none of our methods is sufficient on its own. Experiments isolate the physics, simulation reaches conditions experiments cannot, and full-scale data from industry keeps both honest. The interesting problems usually appear where the three disagree.

Prospective students

We are a CFD-centred ship hydrodynamics group. If you are interested in computational fluid dynamics applied to ships — resistance and hull roughness, seakeeping, manoeuvring — or in marine renewable energy, we would like to hear from you.

Fluid mechanics, numerical methods and some programming are useful. Experience with CFD software (STAR-CCM+, OpenFOAM), Python or MATLAB helps but is not required — curiosity and persistence matter more.

Email s.song@inha.ac.kr with your CV and a short note on what you would like to work on.
